1The idea

Numbers in the examples are made up to show the arithmetic.

A clinician rates ataxia with a scale called SARA. CeMoQu measures movement and voice with a computer, then turns each measurement into a score on the same kind of scale. Higher means more difficulty.

  1. Do a test.
  2. The computer measures one or more things.
  3. Rules turn the measurement into a score.
  4. The score is called the CMQ Score.

A CMQ Score is a research estimate, not a diagnosis. It still has to be compared with clinician-rated SARA scores.

2Line Drawing

You trace a straight line from S to F with your finger. If your hand shakes, the path zigzags across the line.

What is measured

  • Turn: a place where the path changes direction. Swings under 0.25 cm are ignored.
  • Amplitude: how far the path travels between two neighboring turns.

Steps

  1. Find every amplitude in the trial.
  2. Remove the largest 5%, rounded up, so one sudden jump does not decide the score. With fewer than 3 amplitudes, nothing is removed.
  3. Take the mean of the rest.
  4. Change the mean into a score.
Mean amplitudeScore
No turns0
Under 2 cm1
2 cm to under 5 cm2
5 cm or more3
Could not complete the task4

A trial scores 4 with 3 failed starts in a row (SF), 30 seconds without finishing (TO), leaving the screen 3 times or more (OB), or lifting the finger 10 times or more (DC).

Turn amplitudeLD CMQ Score
Ā = mean of Ak, largest 5% removed

Ak is the distance between neighboring turns. Cut-offs of 2 and 5 cm come from the SARA Nose-Finger item.

Hand and itemSARA method
Hand = mean of trials 3, 4, 5  ·  Item = (Right + Left) / 2

Both hands count equally.

Example. The amplitudes are 3.1, 2.8 and 3.4 cm. Remove the largest, 3.4. The mean of 3.1 and 2.8 is 2.95 cm. That is between 2 and 5 cm, so the trial scores 2.
Trials 3, 4 and 5 score 2, 2 and 1, so that hand is (2 + 2 + 1) / 3 = 1.67.

3Random Target

A target appears. You move your finger to it and stop. The score asks how close you stopped.

Steps

  1. Find the fastest moment of the movement.
  2. After that, find the first moment the speed stays at 8% of the top speed or less for at least 0.1 second. That is the stop.
  3. Measure the distance from the fingertip to the center of the target. This is the arrival error, E.
  4. Change E into a score.
Arrival errorScore
Under 1 cm0
1 cm to under 5 cm1
5 cm to under 15 cm2
15 cm or more3
Arrival errorRT CMQ Score
E = ‖ fingertip at stop − target center ‖

Cut-offs of 5 and 15 cm come from the SARA Finger Chase item. The 1 cm limit is provisional.

Hand and itemSARA method
Hand = mean of last 3 tracked  ·  Item = (Right + Left) / 2

SARA rates the mean of the last 3 of 5 movements.

If the camera never sees the hand during a movement, that movement is Missing and is not scored. If 3 or more of 5 are Missing, the hand scores 4.

Example. Five arrival errors are 0.44, 0.86, 1.42, 1.66 and 1.00 cm. Their scores are 0, 0, 1, 1 and 1. The last three give (1 + 1 + 1) / 3 = 1.00. If the left hand is 2.33, the item score is (1.00 + 2.33) / 2 = 1.67 (rounded).

4Speech

You do three tasks: read a passage, repeat “pa-ta-ka”, and hold “ah”. Each task gets a score from 0 to 6. Then the three are combined.

Step 1. Turn each measurement into a level

A level, called severity, runs from 0 (normal) to 6 (most severe). Use the table below. Go left to right and stop at the last step you crossed. If you cross none, the level is 0.

Step 2. Combine the levels into a task score

Each level is multiplied by its share, then added and rounded.

Reading scoreweight 0.50
R = 0.8 × WER level + 0.2 × reading rate level

Word errors carry most of this task because SARA defines the item by how well speech is understood.

Pa-ta-ka scoreweight 0.40
P = 0.4 × rate + 0.3 × rhythm + 0.3 × pause

Slow, uneven and broken repetition each add to the score.

Vowel scoreweight 0.10
V = 0.45 × phonation + 0.35 × F0 + 0.2 × dropout

How long, how steady and how unbroken the voice is.

Step 3. Combine the three tasks

Combined scoreSD CMQ Score
W = 0.5R + 0.4P + 0.1V  ·  Score = round(W)

The three task scores are weighted and rounded.

Reading counts most because SARA defines this item by how well speech is understood. If R is 4 or more, the final score cannot be lower than R.

Reading floorrule
If R ≥ 4: Score = larger of round(W) and R

Other tasks cannot average away a serious intelligibility problem.

Example. Reading: 100 words, 30 wrong. WER is 0.30, past .28, so level 3. The rate is 90 words per minute, below 100, so level 3. R = 0.8 × 3 + 0.2 × 3 = 3.
Pa-ta-ka: rate 3.0 is level 4, rhythm CV 0.25 is level 3, pause ratio 0.40 is level 3. P = 1.6 + 0.9 + 0.9 = 3.4, which rounds to 3.
Vowel: phonation 3.0 s is level 3, F0 CV 0.08 is level 3, 2 dropouts is level 2. V = 1.35 + 1.05 + 0.4 = 2.8, which rounds to 3.
W = 1.5 + 1.2 + 0.3 = 3. R is under 4, so the SD score is 3.
Example with the floor. R = 5, P = 2, V = 1. W = 2.5 + 0.8 + 0.1 = 3.4, which rounds to 3. R is 4 or more, so the SD score is 5.

5Sitting Balance

You sit for 10 seconds with your feet off the ground and your arms out. The camera follows the middle of your shoulders. ST was not part of the simulated verification.

  • Sway: how far the shoulders move from their usual position.
  • Sway duty: the share of time the sway is more than 1 cm.
What happenedScore
Largest sway under 1 cm0
Some sway, duty 60% or less1
Sway more than 60% of the time2

Under 9.5 s, tracking lost, or no data4
Example. The largest sway is 1.23 cm and the duty is 1%. That is more than 1 cm, but sway happens only a little of the time, so the score is 1.

6Errors and artifacts

Real recordings contain jumps, coughs and lost tracking. These rules decide what happens to them.

LD: one large jumpLine Drawing

The largest 5% of amplitudes are removed before the mean is taken, so one sudden movement does not set the score.

RT: hand leaves the cameraRandom Target

If the hand returns, both parts of the path are kept. If it never returns, the last seen point is used when no stop is found. If it is never seen, that movement is Missing.

SD: coughs, sighs and breathsSpeech

Reading: the score uses recognized words, so these sounds are not measured as speech. Pa-ta-ka and vowel: CeMoQu looks for sound with no voice pitch.

Sound with no voice pitchTreated as
0.15 s or longer, and at least 1.3 times as loud as the typical voiceCough
0.3 s or longerBreath or sigh
Anything shorterKept as speech, such as p, t and k

The time of a cough or sigh is removed from the calculation. It is not a syllable, not phonation and not a pause. If these sounds make up more than 40% of the sound in a recording, nothing is removed, because the voice itself may be breathy.

Example. A 10 s pa-ta-ka has 33 syllables and a 0.4 s cough. Rate = 33 / (10 − 0.4) = 3.44 syllables per second, and the cough is not counted as a syllable.

7Why these choices

Some numbers come directly from SARA. Others were set by the CeMoQu team and are provisional.

RuleSource
RT: 5, 15 cm; 5 movements; last 3 averaged; (Right + Left) / 2SARA Finger Chase
LD: 2, 5 cm; (Right + Left) / 2SARA Nose-Finger tremor amplitude
ST: 10 s, feet off the ground, arms out; sway and support levelsSARA Sitting
SD: 0 to 6 scale built around intelligibilitySARA Speech
RT: 1 cm limit for score 0; 50% rule for score 4CeMoQu, provisional
LD: failure limits and the 0.25 cm turn limitCeMoQu, provisional
ST: 1 cm, 60%, 8 cm and 10 cm limitsCeMoQu, provisional
SD: all step values and weights inside each taskCeMoQu, provisional

Same numbers, different test. In SARA a clinician watches the patient follow a finger or point to the nose. CeMoQu measures a reach to a screen target and a traced line. So even where the numbers match, patient data must show whether the results agree.

Why the speech tasks have these weights

  • Reading has the largest weight because SARA defines the speech item by intelligibility (Schmitz-Hübsch 2006).
  • Pa-ta-ka weight was raised because pa-ta features were the strongest group in SARAspeech (Grobe-Einsler 2023).
  • F0 and syllable timing are reported features of ataxic dysarthria (Kent 2000).

These sources guided the choices. They did not calculate the weights.

What this means: the code followed its scoring rules in every simulated scenario. It does not show clinical accuracy, because no patient data were used.

Method

  • Five scenarios were built at every score level: LD 0 to 4, RT 0 to 4, SD 0 to 6.
  • LD and RT inputs ran through the platform scoring code, with raw CSV and trajectory files saved for each trial.
  • RT covers Camera mode. SD measured values ran through the scoring code, with no audio. ST was not evaluated.
  • Scenarios cover ataxia-related features and technical conditions such as calibration error, low frame rate, tracking loss and values near cut-offs.
  • Expected scores were set from the scoring rules when each scenario was designed. A separate Python recalculation from the stored raw files and measures gave the same score in 85 of 85 scenarios.
  • Inputs: 11 scenarios near cut-offs, 10 with failure codes or lost tracking, and 5 with calibration error, low frame rate or partial tracking loss.
  • Limit of the check: the Python recalculation used the same rules, so it tests the code, not the rules. LD failure counts and SD measures came from stored values.

Python recalculation

The scoring rules were written again in a separate Python program. It reads the stored raw files and measures of every simulated scenario and calculates each score again.

Limitations

  • No patient data are included. RT and LD cut-offs follow SARA, but the tasks differ from the clinical exam. Other limits are provisional.
  • A slow return after overshoot can be read as the stop point. In testing, a 3 cm overshoot with a 0.3 s return scored 1 instead of the targeted 0, so scenario SYN-P102 uses a 0.13 s return. This needs checking with real data.

9What we have not proven

These limits matter when reading any CMQ Score.

  • No patient data. All verification results are simulated. Agreement with clinician-rated SARA has not been tested.
  • Same numbers, different test. RT and LD cut-offs follow SARA, but CeMoQu does not use the same task as the clinical exam.
  • Provisional values. SD steps and weights, the RT 1 cm limit, ST limits and LD failure limits are CeMoQu choices.
  • Cough detection is new. It was tested with synthetic coughs and sighs added to a healthy voice. Real coughs, patients with a breathy voice, and whispered speech have not been tested. Speech can still be removed by mistake, and a voiced cough can be missed.
  • Sitting Balance. ST was not part of the simulated verification.
  • Other open questions. Discontinuities may reflect front-back tremor, which a flat screen cannot measure. Test-retest reliability and minimal detectable change are not yet known.

11Words used here

  • WER, word error rate: wrong words divided by the words in the passage. Wrong means missed, changed or added.
  • CV, coefficient of variation: how uneven something is. It is the standard deviation divided by the mean.
  • F0: the basic pitch of the voice.
  • Dropout: a short break in the voice.
  • Phonation: time the voice is sounding.
  • Mean: add the numbers and divide by how many there are.
